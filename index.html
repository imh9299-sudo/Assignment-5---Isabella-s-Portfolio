Collections

This collection brings together digital and traditional works that merge ancestral reference with speculative worldbuilding. The character designs draw from pre-Columbian civilizations — ceremonial dress, ornament, palettes, and motifs — while integrating original fantasy concepts developed for a novel in progress, including invented races, clothing systems, physiognomies, and cosmologies. The same approach informs the landscapes, creatures, and constructed settings, where research-based visual heritage and fictional invention co-exist. Several environments are directly inspired by the cultural and natural landscapes of Guatemala, reflecting both personal origin and the enduring influence of Mesoamerican worldviews on this body of work. The collection positions heritage not as static history, but as a living source for imagining Indigenous futures.

Projects

Evocation 2025

This project began with an unexpected encounter at a wall site, where fragments of a demolished building—glass, sand, seashells, and debris—revealed traces of personal and historical presence rather than absence. Drawn to these remnants, I explored how fragments function as conduits for memory, evoking a sense of the whole without fully reconstructing it. Using materials collected from the site alongside ceramic elements of my own making, I translated selected moments from conversations with former residents and the landlord into visual compositions depicting everyday yet intimate scenes of domestic life, labor, and aspiration. Influenced by practices that merge memory, imagination, and materiality, the work takes the form of a puzzle-like assemblage, reflecting the fragmented nature of memory itself. Rather than offering a complete narrative, the pieces act as material repositories, storing, honoring, and quietly gesturing toward the lives that once inhabited the now-demolished space.

What the Hands Remember 2025

Sensorial memories are a language of their own, spoken through the textures of sand, the scent of spice, the fragrance of perfume, and the gesture of hands. Rooting from a cultural journey of the UAE, these paintings gather the artist’s experience in Abu Dhabi and offer a multi-sensory conversation. Stripped of skin tones, each hand is a vessel of human presence, with a bowl that mirrors the contents grasped, and whispers an invitation for the curious to engage.

These hands collect one of the essence attributes to Abu Dhabi, one of the most abundant substances
While the hands for this painting seem to be holding nothing, the fabric of the canvas is soaked with a rich fragance, a dominant product found across the emirates.
Across the many Souqs found in the emirates, one can find a variety of containers and bags filled with rich scented spices, the third essence chosen to speak for the culture of Abu Dhabi

Design Philosophy

I believe graphic design should communicate ideas with clarity, intention, and visual impact, serving both people and society by making information meaningful and accessible. Designers play a key role in shaping culture and solving problems by identifying the core objective of a project and balancing aesthetics with functionality according to its purpose. Efficient design goes beyond personal preference or trends; it is memorable, purposeful, and, most importantly, clearly understood by its intended audience. Informed by my background and interests in cultural memory and storytelling, I merge research, symbolism, and contemporary design practices to create work that is visually engaging, conceptually grounded, and capable of connecting audiences to ideas, identities, and narratives in a thoughtful and lasting way.

About

Isabella Hernandez is a visual artist from Guatemala who combines digital and traditional techniques to explore the intersection of cultural heritage and fantasy. Her work draws inspiration from pre-Columbian civilizations and her Guatemalan roots, while also incorporating original characters, creatures, and settings developed for a fantasy novel in progress.
